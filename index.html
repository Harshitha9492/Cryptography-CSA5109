Security Objective

End-to-end protection for medical document exchange

Data Integrity

Cryptographic hashes detect any modification to diagnosis records, prescription notes, or lab results while data is transmitted between facilities and portals.

Authentication

Digital signatures allow recipients to verify that the document was created by the legitimate clinician or institution and not by an attacker.

Non-Repudiation

A validated digital signature creates legal evidence that the sender signed the record and cannot plausibly deny it later.

Auditability

Hashes and signatures can be logged, reviewed, and reconciled to support compliance, incident response, and forensics investigations.

Deployment Recommendation

Optimal protocol for secure health information exchange

For an electronic health record ecosystem, the strongest practical deployment is SHA-256 for document hashing combined with ECDSA P-256 or Ed25519 digital signatures, enforced through certificate-based identity management and secure key storage in HSMs. This balance offers fast verification, strong collision resistance, efficient bandwidth use, and reliable non-repudiation without incurring the overhead of legacy RSA key sizes.

  • Hashing: SHA-256 for message integrity across lab, clinician, and insurer records
  • Signing: ECDSA P-256 or Ed25519 for provider authentication and repudiation prevention
  • Key protection: Hardware security modules and PKI certificates
  • Audit and governance: time-stamped logs for forensic review and compliance
